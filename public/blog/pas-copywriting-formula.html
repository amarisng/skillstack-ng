The PAS Formula: A Simple Way to Write Ads That Actually Sell

Most ads fail for the same reason: they describe the product before the reader has any reason to care. PAS fixes that by starting with the reader's problem, not your offer.

What PAS stands for

Problem. Name the specific problem your reader already has — not a made-up one. Agitate. Make the cost of that problem feel real, so ignoring it stops being an option. Solution. Only now do you introduce what you're selling, as the obvious way out.

The order matters. Most Nigerian businesses skip straight to the solution — "Our jollof rice is the best in Lagos!" — and wonder why nobody stops scrolling. Nobody was thinking about jollof rice a second ago. PAS earns their attention first.

Problem: be specific, not generic

"Struggling to grow your business?" is not a problem — it's a category. Everyone is technically struggling with something. Specificity is what makes a reader think "that's me."

Weak: "Are you struggling to get customers?"
Strong: "You post on Instagram every day, but the same 40 people like your photos and none of them buy."

The strong version names a moment the reader has actually lived. That's the test for every Problem line you write: could only someone in this exact situation nod at it?

Agitate: make the cost visible

Agitation isn't about being dramatic — it's about making the reader feel what staying stuck actually costs them, in time, money, or opportunity.

"Every week you don't fix this, you're not just missing sales — you're training your followers to scroll past you. By the time a real customer shows up, they won't even notice."

Nigerian audiences respond especially well to agitation grounded in real stakes: rent, school fees, the cost of ads that aren't converting, competitors who are visibly winning. Vague fear doesn't move anyone. Specific loss does.

Solution: position it as the obvious next step

By the time you reach the Solution, the reader should already want an answer — so this section can be short and direct. Don't re-sell the problem. Just show the bridge.

"That's exactly what our 30-day content calendar template fixes — plug in your brand, and every post is built to convert, not just get likes."

A full example, start to finish

For a Lagos hair salon:

Problem: "You've tried three different hairstylists this year because none of them got your natural hair texture right."

Agitate: "Every bad appointment costs you a Saturday, ₦15k, and another six weeks hiding your hair under a wig while it recovers."

Solution: "GlowUp Salon specialises only in natural hair — book your first appointment this week and get 20% off."

Where to use PAS

  • Facebook and Instagram ad copy
  • WhatsApp broadcast messages to past customers
  • The opening paragraph of a landing page
  • Cold outreach messages to potential clients

It won't fit every piece of writing — a product description doesn't need agitation — but for anything whose job is to make someone act, PAS is one of the most reliable structures there is.
