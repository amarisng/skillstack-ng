How to Open a Sales Conversation Nigerians Actually Respond To

Most sales messages die in the first line. Not because the offer is bad — because the opener gives the reader zero reason to keep reading past "Good day sir/ma."

Why "Good day, I am reaching out to introduce our services" fails

It's not rude, it's just invisible. Every scam, every mass broadcast, and every genuine offer all start the exact same way, so the reader's brain has learned to skip straight past it without registering a word. If your opener sounds like every other cold message someone's received this week, it gets the same response: none.

Weak: "Good day ma, I hope you are doing well. I am reaching out to introduce our digital marketing services which can help grow your business online."

What makes someone actually reply

Three things, almost every time: relevance, specificity, and a low-effort way to respond.

Relevance means the message is clearly about them, not a copy-paste sent to fifty other people. Specificity means naming something real you noticed, not a generic compliment. Low effort means the first reply you're asking for is small — a yes/no, not a meeting booking.

Strong: "Hi Chioma — noticed GlowSkin's Instagram hasn't posted in 3 weeks even though your reviews are great. Is that intentional, or has content just been hard to keep up with?"

Notice what this does: it references something true and specific about her business, it isn't selling anything yet, and it asks one easy question she can answer in five seconds.

Three openers that work across channels

  • The observation opener: Point out something specific and true about their business, then ask a question about it.
  • The mutual-connection opener: "[Name] mentioned you're looking to..." — borrowed trust is one of the strongest openers there is.
  • The value-first opener: Lead with something genuinely useful — a quick tip, an audit, a resource — before asking for anything at all.

What to do with the reply

The opener's only job is to get a reply — not to close the sale. Once someone responds, the conversation moves into discovery: asking questions to understand their actual problem before you say a word about your offer. Pitching too early, right after the first reply, is the second most common way sales conversations die (the first is a weak opener in the first place).

The pattern behind all of it

Every strong opener treats the first message as an invitation to talk, not a sales pitch compressed into one line. Nigerians are not unusually hard to sell to — they're just, like everyone, tired of being sold to before anyone's earned the right to.
